📋 Your brief — read this first

You and Michael are co-leading this call equally. He opens and drives discovery. You carry the second half of discovery and own the offer delivery. The split isn't about seniority — it's about playing to strengths. Your marketing and business background makes you the right voice for the offer.

The client has two founders — Chinwendu (the communicator, she's been speaking to Michael) and Chinwe (her business partner, less visible). You may need to bring Chinwe in directly. Watch for her going quiet.

One thing to hold front of mind: She already told Michael "we don't even have a website." She's half-sold before the call starts. Don't over-explain what she already knows.

What you're working with

Instagram: 217 followers, 107 posts, low engagement. TikTok: 48 followers, but one video hit 10.6k views. Content is active since April/May.

The core diagnosis

Two businesses (lash service + studio rental) sharing one page with no website, no booking system, no reviews. Interest exists. Infrastructure doesn't.

The booking journey

Customer sees content → lands on profile → has to DM → waits → may drop off. WhatsApp link on Facebook doesn't even work. Zero Google reviews.

Comparator to use

The Brow Hut (Abuja): 739 Google reviews, 4.9 stars, live booking calendar. Luxury Winks: dedicated booking site with deposit + cancellation policy.

🚫 Rules for this call
  • Don't speak over Michael during his sections — hold your space and wait for the handoff
  • Don't fill silence with chatter — if a question lands and they pause, let them think
  • Don't pitch before you've confirmed what they said the pain is — anchor the offer to their words
  • Don't over-explain why it's free — say it once, clearly, move on
  • Don't leave without a specific follow-up day — "early next week" is not a next step

How the handoff works

Michael handles Round 1 — the business overview, how it started, which revenue stream is primary. Once he has that answer, he hands to you naturally. Listen for his cue:

🔷 Michael (cue to hand to you)
"That's really helpful context. Teri, did you want to pick up from here?"
Round 2 — The booking journey (your questions)
Teri
"When someone finds you on TikTok or Instagram, what typically happens next — walk me through it from their side."
You want them to describe the broken journey themselves. Don't describe it for them.
Teri
"Roughly how many enquiries do you get in a week? And of those, how many actually end up booking?"
Surfaces the conversion gap. If they don't know the number — that itself is the signal.
Teri
"What happens when you're not available to respond to a DM straight away — do those leads wait or do they move on?"
Gets them to admit they're losing leads. They feel this already. You're just naming it.
What to listen for in round 2
  • Frustration in their voice about missed messages or slow responses
  • Uncertainty about their own conversion numbers — this means they have no system
  • Any mention of clients who "went elsewhere" — that's the emotional hook
Round 3 — Ambition + pain (your questions)
Teri
"Where do you want the business to be in 12 months — more lash clients, more studio renters, or something else entirely?"
Opens up their ambition. Shows you're thinking about their future, not just fixing the present.
Teri
"If you had to name one thing — the one thing that if fixed would make the biggest difference right now — what would that be?"
Golden question. Whatever they say becomes the anchor for your offer. Use their exact words when you pitch.
⚠️
If Chinwe still hasn't spoken by Q6, address her directly: "Chinwe — from your side of things, what's the gap you feel most?" She needs to be part of the yes.

18–27 min
Directional Offer — You own this
Your role: deliver the pitch
▶
How Michael hands to you
🔷 Michael
"That's really helpful — thank you both. Based on what you've just shared, I want to hand to Teri who's going to walk you through what we're thinking."
Step 1 — Always open with this frame (universal)
▶ Teri
"What strikes me about Lush Lash Atelier is that you've already got two things most businesses at your stage are still trying to get — a clear brand and content that's already getting traction. That 10k view on the space tour? That's not luck. That's real interest. The problem is there's nowhere for that interest to land. Someone watches your video, goes to your profile, can't figure out how to book, and moves on. That gap is what we want to close."
💡
Pause here. Let that land. Don't rush into the offer. A beat of silence is fine — it shows confidence.
Step 2 — The offer (choose based on their priority)
Offer A — Lash service focus
▶
"What we want to build for you is a clean, professional website — your services, your pricing, your work, and a way for people to book directly without needing to DM and wait. Linked to your Instagram and TikTok so every piece of content you post drives people somewhere that actually converts. And we set up your Google Business profile properly so when someone in Abuja searches for lash services, you show up — with reviews."

Impact frame: "The Brow Hut in Abuja has 739 Google reviews and a live booking calendar. They're not doing better lashes than you — they just made it easier to find them and easier to say yes. That's the gap we're closing."

Offer B — Studio rental focus
▶
"What we want to build is a dedicated landing page for the studio — clear photos of the space, your pricing tiers, what's included, and a simple booking form. Linked from your TikTok bio and Instagram. Right now your 10k-view video sends people to a profile with 48 followers and a DM instruction. We want to send them somewhere that closes the deal for you while you're sleeping."

Impact frame: "Artists looking for a professional space in Abuja are searching right now. If you're not findable with a credible presence, they book someone else — not because the other space is better, but because they could see it and trust it."

Offer C — Both businesses
▶
"You've got two audiences and they need different things from you online. We want to build a website that serves both — a clean home for the brand, with a section for lash bookings and a section for artists looking to rent. One place that covers both. We start with whichever is the priority for you right now, and we build the second in phase two."

Impact frame: "Right now both audiences are landing on a social page with 48 TikTok followers and a DM instruction. That's not a reflection of the quality of what you've built — it's just a missing foundation. We're offering to build that foundation."

Step 3 — The free framing (say this once, clearly)
▶ Teri
"Quick note on how we're working at this stage — we're offering this at no cost to you. We're building our portfolio of case studies and we're selective about who we work with. We saw something in Lush Lash Atelier. In return, we'd ask for your honest feedback and a testimonial once we've delivered. That's the deal."
🚨
If they push on "why free?" — don't over-explain. Say: "We're at a stage where we want strong case studies more than we want fees. You fit what we're looking for." Then move on.
Step 4 — Check in before you close
▶ Teri
"Does this sound like the right direction for where you want to take the business?"
⚠️
Wait for both of them to respond. Don't accept only Chinwendu nodding. Look at Chinwe directly. If she's hesitant, ask: "Chinwe — does this align with what you'd want to see happen?"
